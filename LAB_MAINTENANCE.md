# 🧹 Lab Maintenance

> Keeping a safe, functional lab requires consistent upkeep. The tasks below are divided by **who is responsible** and **how often they must be completed**. Instructors, TAs, and students share responsibility — confirm your assignment with your TA or instructor at the start of each week.

## 1. Weekly Eye Wash Station Checks

**Frequency:** Every week
**Responsible:** Instructor (Goller) and/or undergraduate researchers

**Purpose:** OSHA and institutional safety standards require eye wash stations to be activated weekly to flush stagnant water and confirm the unit is functional.

**Procedure:**
1. Locate eye wash stations: 1520 has two, 1503 has one, and both the prep lab and instrument room have one each. 
2. Remove the dust cover and set it aside.
3. Activate the unit and allow water to run for a **minimum of 30 seconds**.
4. Confirm the water flow is **strong, steady, and reaches eye level** on both nozzles.
5. Check for any visible damage, corrosion, or obstructions.
6. Deactivate the unit and replace the dust cover.
7. Record the date and your initials in the **Eye Wash Station Log** (hanging near each eye wash station).
8. Report any malfunction immediately to the lab safety officer — do **not** use a malfunctioning station.

> ⚠️ **Do not skip this check.** A faulty eye wash station during a chemical splash is a serious safety risk.

## 2. Incubator Cleanup

**Frequency:** Full cleaning monthly; interior wipe-down after each experimental use  
**Responsible:** Users clean up the incubator they have used after a multi-day experiment

**Purpose:** Contaminated incubators spread organisms across experiments and degrade incubator performance.

### After every experimental session (undergraduate researcher/TA responsibility)
1. Remove all your cultures, plates, and tubes immediately after incubation.
2. If any spills occurred: wipe up immediately with 10% bleach solution, followed by 70% ethanol.
3. Discard any unlabeled or abandoned materials into biohazard waste.

### Monthly deep clean (undergraduate researchers with help from instructors)
1. **Power down and unplug** the incubator or switch to the backup unit.
2. Remove all racks and trays; carefully clean with 70% ethanol, then rinse with dH₂O and air dry.
3. Wipe interior walls, ceiling, and floor with 70% ethanol; allow 15 min contact time.
4. Rinse all surfaces with dH₂O to remove residue.
5. Wipe dry; replace racks and trays.
6. Reconnect power and allow the incubator to re-equilibrate for ≥ 1 h before use.
7. Record the date, your initials, and any issues in the **Incubator Maintenance Log** (posted on the incubator door).

## 3. Collect and Label Autoclave Waste

**Frequency:** Every lab session (end of session)  
**Responsible:** TA; students responsible for preparing their own waste bags

**Purpose:** Proper collection and labeling ensure safe, compliant autoclaving of biohazardous materials and protect facility staff.

**What goes in autoclave waste:**
- Used agar plates and broth cultures
- Contaminated pipette tips and microfuge tubes (in autoclave-safe bags)
- Used glass slides with biological material
- Any liquid biological waste (in sealed, autoclave-safe bottles)

**Procedure:**
1. Collect biohazard bags from each bench; ensure bags are no more than **¾ full** and securely tied.
2. Attach an **autoclave waste label** (supply drawer, bench C) to each bag. Fill in:
   - Date
   - Contents (e.g., "LB plates + *E. coli*")
   - Lab section and room number
   - Responsible TA initials
3. Place labeled bags in the designated autoclave cart in the prep room (Room 101).
4. For liquid waste: ensure bottle caps are **loosened one half-turn** before autoclaving (never fully sealed).
5. Complete the **Autoclave Waste Log** (prep room binder), recording date, number of bags, volume of liquid waste, and TA initials.
6. Autoclave on the **gravity cycle: 121 °C, 15 psi, 30 min** (solid/dry waste) or **liquid cycle: 121 °C, 15 psi, 45 min** (liquid waste).
7. Allow bags to cool; verify the autoclave indicator tape has changed color, then dispose per institutional waste guidelines.

> ♻️ **Never place sharps (needles, broken glass) in standard autoclave bags.** Use the dedicated sharps container.

## 4. Organize Lab Spaces

**Frequency:** End of every lab session; thorough reorganization weekly  
**Responsible:** All students and TAs

**Purpose:** A well-organized lab prevents accidents, reduces lost materials, and ensures the next section can start on time.

### End-of-session cleanup (every session)
1. Return all shared reagents, stock solutions, and equipment to their labeled locations.
2. Wipe down your bench with 70% ethanol; allow to air dry.
3. Ensure all pipettes are returned to holders at the **minimum volume setting** (to preserve spring tension).
4. Dispose of all liquid waste in the appropriate containers (bleach waste vs. general drain-safe).
5. Replace lids on all reagent bottles and confirm they are tightly sealed.
6. Push stools under benches and clear all walkways.
7. Turn off Bunsen burners; confirm gas valves are closed.

### Weekly reorganization (TA responsibility — Fridays or before Monday sections)
1. Audit all shared supply drawers: restock tips, tubes, and disposable gloves as needed. Record restock in the **Supply Log** (bench C drawer).
2. Check expiration dates on buffers, antibiotics, and stains; flag or discard expired materials per the disposal SOP.
3. Confirm all equipment (centrifuges, vortexers, heat blocks) is clean and returned to assigned locations.
4. Sweep and mop the lab floor.
5. Verify that all hazard labels on chemical bottles are legible; replace faded labels immediately.
6. Report any broken or damaged equipment to the lab coordinator using the **Equipment Issue Form** (posted in prep room and on the LMS).
